# Evidence method

## Frame before searching

Write down:

- the bounded question;
- which verdict, scope boundary, or decision it could change;
- relevant source classes;
- major representatives within each class;
- the stopping condition.

Research without decision impact is background reading, not a standardization ticket.

## Judge sources

Prefer the source that owns a claim. Evaluate each source for authority, relevance, independence, currency, and directness. Record publication and observation/access dates when change over time matters.

Current practice is evidence of behavior, not automatic normative authority. Popularity is not consensus. Silence is not agreement.

## Compare

Use a compact matrix:

| Representative | Source class | Shared purpose | Approach | Agreement | Divergence | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover all major representatives that could materially change the answer without attempting an exhaustive census. Actively seek at least one credible counterexample to an emerging pattern.

Separate:

- direct facts;
- interpretations supported by those facts;
- normative implications that require a decision.

## Stop

Stop at saturation when further sampling is unlikely to change feasibility, scope, or a pending decision. Continue only when a named gap has plausible decision value.

State uncovered representatives, weak source classes, conflicting observations, and what evidence would change the conclusion.

## Curate

Use claim-level citations. Keep excerpts short. Correct or remove invalid evidence; amend incomplete active research. Preserve older material only when it remains necessary to explain a live or superseding decision.
